# Coming up for replacement

Water heaters, fridges, laptops and roofs all wear out eventually, and they're rarely cheap. Ferret estimates when
each thing is likely to need replacing, so the big expenses don't come as a surprise.

These are **estimates** to plan with, not advice. Plenty of things last much longer than usual, and nothing here
tells you to replace something just because it's old.

## How Ferret works it out

For each thing:

- **How long it should last**, the first of:
  - what you set on the thing itself (**Edit → Lasts (years)**),
  - what you set on its category or a parent category (**Categories & tags → edit a category → Things in it last**),
  - the typical life of its kind, which Ferret knows for about 65 kinds of household thing: a water heater
    about 10 years, a fridge 13, a dishwasher 10, a furnace 18, a roof 22, a laptop 5, a TV 8. It goes by the
    thing's name, then its category's.
- **When its life started**: the day it was bought, or, if you didn't record that, the day it was added to Ferret.
- **What replacing it might cost**: its *To replace new* price if you set one, otherwise what you paid, otherwise
  what it's worth, times how many there are.

Accessories and supplies (a TV stand, filters, bags, blades) and [parts of other things](./stock) don't get a
typical life of their own. Neither do boxes, or things you no longer have.

## On a thing's page

Under its purchase details: *Typical life of a refrigerator: about 13 years · Likely due around 2034 · about $1,500 to
replace*. Once it's past that: *Past its typical life*. If the kind is wrong, or yours is built to last, set
**Lasts (years)** on it.

## Coming up for replacement

**Coming up for replacement** (in the menu under Insurance, or **See all** on the dashboard card) lists what's past
its typical life, then each of the next ten years, with what replacing those things might cost in total. The
dashboard shows the next five years at a glance.

Things Ferret has no typical life for are counted at the bottom. Give their category a lifespan to include them.

[Claude](./claude) can answer *"what big expenses are coming up?"* from the same forecast.
