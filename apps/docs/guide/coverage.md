# Insurance coverage

A homeowner's or renter's policy covers your belongings up to one amount — and often much less for some kinds of
things: jewelry and watches (theft is often limited to $1,500), firearms, silverware, cash. **Insurance coverage** in
the menu puts your policy's limits next to what your things are worth, so you can see a gap before you need to
claim.

## Adding your limits

The numbers are on your policy's **declarations page**. Start from one of the usual ones — **Personal property
(Coverage C)**, **Jewelry & watches**, **Firearms**… — or **Something else**, and give:

- **Covered up to** — the limit.
- **It covers** — everything, the things in a **category** (Electronics), or the things with a **tag** (Jewelry). Tag the
  things a special limit is about.
- **At** — one property, if you have more than one and the policy is for one.

## Reading it

Each limit shows what the things it covers are worth (their value, or what you paid, times how many) against the limit.
**Over the limit** is red, says by how much, and shows in the bell — that's when to ask your agent about a rider
(scheduled personal property) or a higher limit. Things with no value aren't counted, so a total may be low; the
[insurance report](./insurance-report) lists them.
