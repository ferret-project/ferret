# Maintenance

**Maintenance** keeps the jobs that come round again: *replace the furnace filter every 3 months*, *service the
mower every spring*, *clean the dryer vent every year*. A task can be on an item, on a place, or on the whole household,
and can repeat or be a one-off.

## Common house tasks {#starter}

Not sure where to start? **Common house tasks** on the Maintenance screen (also offered when you add a home in
**Places**) is a checklist of the usual upkeep: testing smoke and CO alarms, the furnace / AC filter and servicing,
gutters, the roof, the dryer vent, flushing the water heater, the sump pump, outdoor faucets before a freeze, and more.
Each shows how often it's usually done. Tick what applies — nothing is added until you do — and each becomes an
ordinary task you can change or remove. Seasonal ones are first due at the start of spring (April 1) or fall
(October 1) — twice-yearly ones at whichever comes first; the rest one interval from today. Tasks you already have
aren't offered again ("Clean gutters" counts as "Clean the gutters").

With more than one property, tasks added right after adding a home carry its name ("Clean the gutters — Lake house");
or pick one of its places for them.

Tap **Done** when it's done: say when, who did it and what it cost. The next due date moves on from the day it was
actually done — so a job done late doesn't come due again too soon. A one-off is finished once it's done.

Each task keeps its history, and the item's page shows its maintenance.

## By the month {#month}

Switch **List** to **Month** to see the tasks on a calendar, so you can spot a busy week before it arrives. Each task
sits on its next due date. Overdue ones sit on today. Later repeats are shown faded with a dashed outline: they're
where the task will land if each one before it is done on time, and they move when you mark one done. A date counted
from use (miles or hours) has a **~** in front of it. Tap a day to see its tasks below the calendar, with **Done**;
tap it again, or **Show the whole month**, for the whole month's list. Ferret remembers which view you used last on
each device.

To have maintenance in Apple, Google or another calendar app, subscribe to its [calendar feed](./reminders#calendar). It
shows the same expected repeats.

## By use {#by-use}

Some upkeep goes by how much something's used, not the calendar: *change the oil every 5,000 miles*, *service the
mower every 50 hours*, *descale every 300 cycles*.

1. On the thing's page, **Track miles / hours** — say what it counts (miles, km, hours or cycles) and what it says now.
2. In its maintenance task, turn on **By use** and say how much — or **Also by use** for a task that repeats by time
   too: whichever comes first (*every 5,000 miles or 6 months*).
3. Now and then, **log a reading** on its page (or ask Claude: *"the car's at 42,180 miles"*).

From two readings at least a week apart Ferret works out how fast it's used and estimates the date — the list says
**Due around** — and that date is what reminders and the calendar use. A reading past the mark makes it due from that
day. Until there's a rate it checks in a year after the last reading. **Done** asks what it says now; the next one is
due that much further on.

## Parts it uses

A task can use up something you keep in stock — *Replace furnace filter* uses one *Furnace filter 20×25×1*. Set it in
the task under **Uses a part**, with how many each time. Then:

- **Done** takes them out of stock (untick **Took … from stock** if you cleaned the old one instead);
- the list shows how many are left and, when they won't last the year, **buy more before** the first time they run
  out. Ferret counts forward through the task's repeats and every other task that uses the same part. One filter
  covers the next change, not the one three months later;
- in the [Month view](#month), a cart marks each time there won't be enough;
- if there aren't enough for the next time, the task shows up in the bell **a month ahead** instead of a week —
  *Due in 12 days — no Furnace filter left* — and the part goes on the [shopping list](./stock#shopping-list), so
  there's time to order. The same goes for a part shared by two tasks that are both due within the month, when there
  are only enough for one.

## From a manual

Upload a product's manual as a document and Ferret may suggest the maintenance it recommends. These collect under
**Suggested maintenance**, at the top of the Maintenance screen and on the thing's page: **Add** opens the task filled in,
to adjust; **Not needed** is remembered. Nothing is added until you tap it. Ferret can also suggest the
[typical upkeep](./ai#suggested-maintenance) for things like yours.

## Service providers

**Service providers** (in the menu) are the people who work on your home — a plumber, the HVAC company, an appliance
repair shop — with their phone, email and website. A task can say who usually does it, and each time it's done can
say who did it and what it cost, so you can see what you spend with whom.
