# Collections

Coins, dolls, trading cards, records, LEGO — a **collection** keeps a set together: what you have, what each is worth,
the details that matter for that kind of thing, and a **want list** of the ones you're still looking for.

## Starting one

**Collections** (in the menu, under Organise) → **Start a collection**. Give it a name (*Morgan silver dollars*), say
what it's of — a [template](./categories-and-tags#templates) like **Coin** or **Trading card** — and, if you like, a few
words about it. The template's details (a coin's year, mint mark and grade) are the collection's columns, and
everything you add to it starts from that template.

## Adding to it

On the collection, **+ Add**: **I have it** or **I'm looking for it**, its name, the template's details, how many, and
what it's worth. Tick **Add another after this one** to go through a stack quickly. You can also put any thing in a
collection from its editor (**Collection**, and **On the want list**).

## The collection's page

- **Have** and **Want list** — what you own, and what you're after.
- **Photos** or **Table** — the table has a column for each of the template's details.
- At the top: how many you have (and pieces, counting duplicates), what they're worth and what you paid, how many are
  wanted, and how complete it is.

## The want list

Things on the want list aren't yours yet, so they stay out of everything that counts what you own: your totals, the
[insurance report](./insurance-report), stock and search. Their value is what they usually go for. When you find one,
**Got it** (on the want list or the thing's page) moves it into what you have — its history says when.

Removing a collection keeps the things you own (just not in a collection any more); its want list goes to
[Recently deleted](./recently-deleted).

Claude can see your collections too: *"which Morgan dollars am I still missing?"*, *"add the 1884-O to my Morgans."*
